01 — How it worksTeach for twenty, learn for forty.

Every guest is both a teacher and a learner. When you arrive, pin your skill to the swap board — anything from sharpening knives to filling in tax forms — and pick two things you'd like to learn.

The three hosted tables run on the hour. In between, the hall is open for one-on-one swaps, tea, and the lost-and-found of good ideas. Children are welcome with a grown-up.

02 — ActivitiesThree tables, all afternoon.

Each table is led by a neighbor and runs three short sessions. Drop in to any of them; places are limited only by elbow room.

  • 1:15 · 2:30 · 3:45

    Bike fix-up bench

    Patch a tube, true a wheel and tune your brakes with Dara from the corner shop. Stands and tools provided.

    Bring: your bike, if it fits through a door

  • 1:30 · 2:45 · 4:00

    Mend & make do

    Darning, buttons, hems and visible patches. The sewing circle brings machines, thread and an endless supply of patience.

    Bring: one garment that needs rescuing

  • 1:00 · 2:15 · 3:30

    Bread for beginners

    Mix, knead and shape a simple loaf, then take home a jar of sourdough starter from the hall's own culture.

    Bring: an apron and a clean jar

03 — AccessGetting in, getting comfortable.

We want everyone at the tables. If something here doesn't work for you, tell us when you save your place and we'll sort it out.

  • EntryStep-free main entrance on Elm Street with an automatic door. The whole event is on the ground floor.
  • ToiletsAccessible, gender-neutral toilet next to the kitchen, with a baby-changing table.
  • SeatingChairs with arms at every table, and a quiet room with soft lighting off the main hall.
  • Getting hereBus 14 stops outside (Elm & 3rd). Bike racks by the door; two blue-badge parking bays.
  • HearingHearing loop in the main hall. Each table has printed step-by-step cards.
  • FoodTea, coffee and snacks are free. Ingredients are labelled, and gluten-free options are available.

04 — RSVPSave your place.

It's free, but knowing numbers helps us set out enough chairs, flour and inner tubes. One form per household is fine.

Only used to send a reminder the day before.

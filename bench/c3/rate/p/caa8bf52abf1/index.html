01 — Introduction

Research with people, not about them.

For ten years I have run usability research with blind, low-vision, Deaf, motor-impaired and neurodivergent participants. I pay them properly, recruit outside the usual panels, and let their sessions set the priorities.

My clients get findings that hold up in a design review: clips, severity ratings tied to real tasks, and a remediation plan sized for the team that will do the work.

02 — Selected case studies

Three engagements, three kinds of evidence.

  1. i.

    Rebuilding a benefits claim form

    Public sector · 14 weeks

    A national benefits service saw screen-reader users abandon its claim form at twice the average rate. I ran 24 moderated sessions across assistive technologies and traced most failures to three patterns: unannounced errors, timed sessions and a custom date picker.

    The team replaced the picker with plain inputs, added error summaries and removed the timeout for saved drafts.

    Completion, AT users
    41% → 88%
    Participants
    24
  2. ii.

    Captions people actually trust

    Streaming media · 9 weeks

    A video platform wanted to know why Deaf and hard-of-hearing subscribers rated its captions poorly despite passing compliance checks. Diary studies with 18 participants showed that timing drift and missing speaker labels mattered far more than accuracy scores.

    I wrote a caption quality rubric the vendor team now uses for acceptance testing.

    Caption complaints
    −63%
    Diary entries
    540
  3. iii.

    A design system that leads with access

    Fintech · 6 months, part-time

    A growing banking app needed its component library to stop shipping inaccessible patterns into every new feature. I audited 62 components, tested the riskiest with keyboard and switch users, and paired with engineers on fixes.

    Each component now ships with documented accessible behaviour and a test that runs in CI.

    Components fixed
    47 of 62
    New AT defects
    Down 70%

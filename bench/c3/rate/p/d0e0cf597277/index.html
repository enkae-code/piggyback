01 · The day

One Saturday, no money changing hands.

Everyone arrives with one thing they can show someone else: fixing a bike chain, a soup recipe, a card trick, how to file a repair request with the city. Swaps run in 30-minute slots at tables around the lawn. You can sign up to teach, just come to learn, or move between both.

Bring a dish to share if you like. The long table goes up at 1, and the day winds down with a group photo at 4:30.

02 · Make, trade, play

Three ways to spend the day.

  • Swap tables11–3

    Teach one, learn one

    Pick a table, take a half-hour slot, and show three or four people how you do the thing you're good at. The board by the gate lists who's teaching what and when.

    BringWhatever your skill needs: tools, ingredients, a deck of cards.

  • Fix-it corner11–4

    Mend something lopsided

    Volunteer fixers help you work out a wobbly chair, a torn jacket, a lamp that flickers, or a bike that clicks. You do the fixing; they talk you through it.

    BringOne broken item you can carry. Bikes are fine.

  • Long table1–5

    Eat outside together

    A shared potluck along one long table, with lawn games after for anyone who still has energy. Water, plates and compost bins are provided.

    BringA dish to share, with a card listing what's in it.

03 · Getting there

Getting there and getting around.

Step-free routes

The Hollis Street gate has a paved, level path straight to the lawn. Every swap table has room for a wheelchair, and you can roll right up to the long table.

Transit and parking

Bus 14 stops at Hollis & 3rd, a two-minute walk away. There are four accessible parking spaces at the gate, and the bike racks sit inside the fence.

Restrooms and rest

The accessible restrooms are in the rec center, 50 m from the lawn. A shaded quiet tent with chairs stays open all day for anyone who needs a break.

Language and hearing

Volunteers who speak Spanish and Vietnamese wear yellow badges. Ask for ASL interpretation when you save your place and we'll book it.

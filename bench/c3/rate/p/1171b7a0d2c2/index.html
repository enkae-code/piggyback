Feature · Adaptive reuse

A roof worth keeping

The Harrow Lane market hall lost its stalls, its trade and its name. The timber overhead is the reason it is still standing.

  • Words by Mara Osei-Lindqvist
  • Drawing by Tomas Reyna
  • Nine minutes to read

For most of its life the Harrow Lane market hall sold fish on Tuesdays and everything else on Saturdays. When the last trader left in 2019, the council's survey recommended demolition and listed the roof as its only asset worth salvaging: forty tonnes of pitch pine, priced by the cubic foot.

Ilse Varga read the same survey and drew the opposite conclusion. Her practice took the lease for a peppercorn on the condition that the building stay open to the street, and spent its first year doing almost nothing but cleaning. The lantern1 was reglazed, the gutters relined, and the tie beam2 left exactly where the Victorians bolted it.

Everything below that beam is now furniture. Workbenches roll; shelving is freestanding against the brick end wall3; the only thing fixed to the floor4 is a drain. A furniture maker rents the north bay, a bicycle co-op the south, and on alternate Thursdays the middle clears for a supper club that seats a hundred and ten.

Varga is wary of calling this a design. “The roof had already solved the hard problem,” she says. “A clear span with daylight from above. Our job was to stop anyone filling it in.”

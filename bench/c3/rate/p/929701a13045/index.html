Buildings of Weir Street, part three

The depot that waited for a train

The last passenger left in 1951. The freight shed on Weir Street is still waiting, and still useful.

Words by Hollis Marrow Drawing by Ada Kettering 14 November 2026 Nine minutes to read

Nobody in Alder Falls remembers the depot being new. It went up in the summer of 1887, a hundred and some feet of white pine on a granite sill, built by the Valley & Northern to take in milled lumber and send out everything a growing town could not make for itself. The railroad lasted sixty-four years. The building has now outlived it by seventy-five.

The reason is less romantic than people want it to be. The sill was laid from the same quarry stone as the mill race,1 eighteen inches above the spring flood line, and it has never once taken water. Above it the shed is a single open room, and a single open room can become almost anything.

It has been a feed store, a roller rink for one ill-advised winter, a polling place, a lumber yard again, and since 1994 the home of the Thursday market. Each tenant left the long eaves alone.2 They throw rain eleven feet clear of the wall and keep the platform3 dry enough to sell bread on in November.

The rails4 are the last thing anyone would expect to find. Two short lengths survive at the south end, rolled in Pennsylvania in 1886 and still spiked to oak ties that the town has twice voted to keep. Ask the market manager why, and you will get a shrug and the same answer every time: the building is still expecting someone.

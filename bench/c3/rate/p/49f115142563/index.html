  1. 01

    Renewing a bus pass without help

    A regional transport authority wanted to know why older and blind residents kept phoning in to renew concessionary passes online.

    I ran 18 sessions in people's homes, on their own devices and with their own screen readers and magnifiers. The form itself was fine. The real barrier was a photo upload step that timed out without warning and never told anyone what had gone wrong.

    Client
    West Riding Transport
    Method
    In-home contextual study
    Outcome
    Phone renewals down 41%

  2. 02

    Medication reminders for people with tremor

    A health start-up's reminder app had good audit scores, but people with Parkinson's were still missing doses.

    Over a three-week diary study, I found that swipe-to-confirm gestures and small snooze targets caused accidental dismissals. We tested larger hold-to-confirm controls with the same participants before anything went to engineering.

    Client
    Dosewell Health
    Method
    Diary study, co-design
    Outcome
    Missed confirmations halved

  3. 03

    Reading a tenancy agreement, plainly

    A housing charity asked whether its digital tenancy pack worked for tenants with learning disabilities and low literacy.

    I ran paired sessions with tenants and their support workers, then rewrote the key clauses with them. The Easy Read version became the default, and the legal text moved behind it, available whenever anyone needs it.

    Client
    Common Ground Homes
    Method
    Paired testing, content co-writing
    Outcome
    Adopted across 4,200 homes

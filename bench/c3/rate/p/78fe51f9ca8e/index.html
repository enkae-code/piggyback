The thick wall comes back

In an old bathhouse on Mercer Row, mass and shade do the work machines used to.

Words by Adaeze Morrow Drawing by Pell Hartigan Nine minutes to read

For eighty years the Mercer Row baths were cooled by nothing but their own walls. Three feet of brick, laid in lime, soaked up the afternoon and gave it back at night. When the city shut the pool in 1994 it fitted fans, then ducts, then a chiller the size of a van, and the building that had once been the coolest room in the district became one of its most expensive to run.

Nadia Ferrante's practice won the conversion with a proposal that read, to the committee, like a refusal. Keep the wall.1 Take the machines out. Open the vault and let the room breathe the way it was drawn to.

The three round openings2 in the roof were bricked up in the fifties. Reopened and glazed, they throw slow discs of light that cross the reading tables over the course of a day. The pool was not demolished but filled with sand and floored in larch,3 so a later generation can dig it out again.

The windows sit at the back of reveals deep enough to sit in,4 and on hot days most people do. Last August, with the street at 34 degrees, the reading room held at 23 without a single fan running.
